To turn the massive pillar of Global Economics, Trade & Finance into a clean, queryable data architecture, we need to split it into distinct, non-overlapping domains.The 5 major sub-topics that cover the complete lifecycle of global wealth, commerce, and monetary policy are structured below.

1. National Macroeconomic Indicators & Fundamentals This sub-topic establishes the baseline health of individual economies. It tracks the domestic metrics that dictate how a country interacts with the rest of the world.Economic Output: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth rates, GDP per capita (purchasing power parity), and gross national income.  Price Stability & Employment: Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation rates, producer price trends, and labor market metrics (unemployment, underemployment).Fiscal Performance: Government revenues, public spending, debt-to-GDP ratios, and sovereign credit ratings.

2. International Trade & Cross-Border Commerce This domain tracks the actual physical and digital movement of goods and services across sovereign boundaries, focusing on real-world supply chains.  Trade Balances: Total value of imports versus exports, distinguishing between merchandise trade (physical goods) and services (software, tourism, consulting).Trade Policy & Restrictions: Tariff rates, import quotas, customs compliance data, and the tracking of bilateral or multilateral free trade agreements.  Logistics & Flow Optimization: Global supply chain bottlenecks, shipping costs, and bilateral trade dependencies between trading partners.

3. Global Financial Markets, Capital Flows & Assets This shifts the focus from physical goods to liquid capital, tracking how money moves globally to seek investment returns across international boundaries.  Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Long-term capital inflows and outflows used to build factories, infrastructure, or acquire foreign enterprises.Portfolio Investment & Equities: Cross-border tracking of stock market performance, global equity indices, and sovereign/corporate bond markets.Banking & Credit Networks: International interbank lending volume, cross-border remittances, and global liquidity indicators.

4. Foreign Exchange (FX) & Monetary Systems This sub-topic monitors the relative value of currencies and the central bank mechanics that control global money supply.Exchange Rate Dynamics: Spot and forward exchange rates, real effective exchange rates (REER), and volatility tracking between major global currency pairs.Central Bank Interventions: Benchmark interest rate decisions, quantitative easing/tightening programs, and open-market operations.International Reserves: The accumulation and composition of foreign currency reserves, gold holdings, and Special Drawing Rights (SDRs).

5. Balance of Payments & Global Economic Governance This aggregate sub-topic acts as the ultimate double-entry bookkeeping ledger for entire nations, alongside the international institutions that enforce financial stability.The Accounts Ledger:Current Account: Tracking trade balances, net primary income (earnings on foreign investments), and secondary income (transfers/remittances).Capital & Financial Accounts: Recording net changes in national ownership of foreign assets.Global Structural Stability: Monitoring sovereign balance-of-payments crises, structural adjustment data, and macroprudential risk indicators.Institutional Frameworks: Multilateral data points originating from systemic economic organizations (e.g., International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, World Trade Organization (WTO), and Bank for International Settlements (BIS)).The Analytical Advantage: Separating the pillar into these five areas allows you to build data pipelines where a change in Sub-topic 4 (a sudden interest rate hike by the US Federal Reserve) can be modeled to show its direct domino effect on Sub-topic 3 (capital outflows from emerging market equity portals) and Sub-topic 5 (current account deficits).
